STAGE INSTALLATIONS · WATER SHOWS

How to procure a water stage and drone show

Separate the brief into surface platforms, aerial drone operations, and lighting or water-effect integration. Scenic lakes, waterfront night tours, and opening events may all be suitable, but unit counts, price, schedule, and performance should not be promised before the site, operating limits, approvals, and maintenance responsibilities are defined.

Prepare the site brief View stage capabilities
DEFINE THE SCOPE

One “drone show” brief may contain three different systems

The RFQ should state whether equipment operates on the water or in the air, and whether it must synchronize with a stage, water screen, fountain, laser, performers, or an existing show-control system.

SURFACE

Floating platform or unmanned surface craft

These units navigate on water or carry lighting, water effects, scenic forms, or compact show elements. Verify buoyancy, stability, propulsion, sealing, communications, recovery points, and water rescue.

AERIAL

Aerial drone light show

These fleets form programmed images above a venue. Verify the operating entity, pilots, airspace and flight permissions, flight and audience separation, communications redundancy, lost-link procedures, and rehearsals.

INTEGRATED

Water-to-sky show system

This combines surface units, aerial fleets, lighting, water effects, and audio through timecode. Verify control protocols, content versions, triggers, emergency stops, and the handoff between suppliers.

VERIFIABLE INPUTS

Replace an effect image with specifications that can be signed off

There are no universal values for the items below. Require written parameters, test conditions, evidence, and limitations for the proposed model, show profile, and site.

Water and shoreline

Normal and extreme water levels, usable depth, wind and wave conditions, current, shoreline access, launch and lifting points, viewing distance, navigation routes, and underwater obstacles.

Structure and equipment

Dimensions, mass and load, buoyancy and stability calculations, materials and corrosion protection, ingress-rating evidence, maintenance access, towing, and manual recovery.

Positioning and control

Unit count, positioning method, tested communication conditions, route or flight boundary, geofencing, lost-link logic, emergency stop, manual takeover, timecode, and DMX or Art-Net interfaces.

Power and endurance

Voltage, rated load, charging or shore-power requirements, endurance under the specified show profile, low-power response, waterproof connectors, electrical protection, and backup power.

Content and rehearsals

Show duration, number of scenes or routes, revision rounds, music-rights responsibility, daytime positioning tests, night rehearsals, weather alternatives, and premiere support.

Handover documents

Detailed drawings, equipment schedule, control topology, risk assessment, operating manual, inspection sheet, spares list, training record, acceptance criteria, and fault-response boundary.

BUDGET AND SCHEDULE

Request a cost breakdown and milestone plan

A fixed price or delivery date is not evidence-based before the survey, content scope, and equipment list exist. Separate equipment, content, engineering, approval support, on-site execution, and maintenance.

Cost drivers

Surface or aerial unit count, custom forms, lighting and water effects, show duration and revisions, survey, freight and lifting, power, permits, insurance, crew, rehearsals, spares, and maintenance.

Delivery milestones

Site confirmation, concept and budget boundary, structural and control development, prototype or critical-part validation, factory testing, installation, daytime tests, night rehearsals, acceptance, and training.

Maintenance budget

Routine inspections, hull and sealing checks, battery health, propulsion or flight units, communications and positioning, lighting and water effects, software backups, spares, seasonal shutdown, and staff training.

SAFETY LIMITS

Water-resistant is not all-weather, and automated is not unattended

Wind, rain, lightning, visibility, water level, communications, and crowd density can change the safe operating envelope. The supplier should state model-specific stop-show criteria, recovery procedures, manual takeover, and rescue arrangements. The on-site lead must be able to cancel or terminate a show when conditions are outside those limits.

For aerial drone fleets, confirm the current rules for registration, operator and pilot qualifications, controlled airspace, operations over people, night operations, and local event approvals in the project country. In the United States, for example, the FAA states that airspace authorization is still required for controlled-airspace operations and that operations over people and at night are subject to specific conditions.
